Behind the Shelter Doors

How do shelter outcomes differ for dogs and cats across Australia’s RSPCA network?

Australia’s shelter system is not a single, uniform network. This project follows how outcomes differ for dogs and cats in the RSPCA network. Two clearly separated case studies then examine Victorian foster care and seasonal outcomes in Townsville.

THE BIG PICTURE · WHAT WE LEARNED

Different places, different recorded outcomes

These animal shelter records cannot be reduced to one count or one outcome rate. Read together, these charts point to three important findings.

01 / OUTCOMES

Recorded outcomes differ by species

In RSPCA's 2024–25 reporting, rehoming accounted for 64.1% of recorded cat outcomes, while reunification accounted for 24.8% of dog outcomes. Looking only at the overall rate hides these differences.

02 / PLACE AND TIME

Where and when we count matters

Reported caseload and outcome composition vary by jurisdiction. Five-year RSPCA trends also depend on reporting coverage, while the monthly Townsville series offers a local view of seasonality rather than a national pattern.

03 / CARE BEYOND ADOPTION

Foster care is part of the wider system

Victoria's 2025 shelter and pound data reveal foster placements beyond the RSPCA outcome summaries. These are placement episodes, not counts of unique animals, and cannot be combined directly with RSPCA caseloads.

Takeaway: Animal welfare cannot be ranked from these counts alone. Consider outcome shares, reporting scope and local conditions together before comparing performance.

SOURCES · METHOD · ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS

Data, definitions and limitations

This page draws on three distinct reporting scopes. Each visualisation identifies its own period and denominator; figures from different organisations have not been added together.

Original data sources

  • RSPCA Australia — Annual Statistics2024–25 outcomes and euthanasia reasons; archive of 2020–21 to 2023–24 for historical trends (Charts 1, 2, 4–10, 13).
  • ABS — ASGS Edition 3 digital boundaries2021 states and territories used for geographic context (Maps 1–3).
  • RSPCA — Shelters and services by jurisdictionDirectory to state and territory information; Chart 3 maps selected verified RSPCA-operated facilities, not a complete national census.
  • Animal Welfare Victoria — 2025 shelter and pound datasetOfficial reporting-entity foster-placement counts, January–December 2025 (Charts 11–12).
  • Townsville City Council — Animals Adopted and ReclaimedMonthly observations, 2021–2025 full years, used to calculate mean values for each calendar month (Chart 14).

How the figures were prepared

Outcome groups. “Reunited” combines Reclaimed and Returned. The outcome share for a species or jurisdiction is its category count divided by that group's recorded total. “In Care” is an ongoing status, not a final exit outcome.

Live-release rate. RSPCA eligible outcomes are used as the denominator; rehomed, reunited and transferred are counted in the live-release numerator. It is not a percentage of all animals entering care.

Time comparisons. Charts 8, 9 and 13 use six selected RSPCA jurisdictions (ACT, QLD, SA, TAS, VIC, WA). NSW and NT are excluded due to changes in reporting coverage. Chart 9 ranks year-on-year percentage change; Chart 13 reports percentage-point differences.

Monthly summary. Chart 14 averages each calendar month's Adopted and Reclaimed observations over five complete years. These figures are Townsville-specific and the source does not distinguish dog versus cat records.

Limits on interpretation

RSPCA figures describe the reporting RSPCA network, not every Australian animal shelter or council pound. Reporting footprints, contracts, policies and category definitions can change between jurisdictions or years.

Victoria's foster data refer to the state's reporting shelters and pounds, and count placements, not unique animals. The Chart 11 top-12 facilities are a subset of Victorian reports.

The Townsville series is a single local centre. An apparent monthly peak cannot establish a nationwide seasonal trend, or show why numbers change. The three datasets are not directly additive.
