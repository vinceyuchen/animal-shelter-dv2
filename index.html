Behind the Shelter Doors

How do shelter outcomes differ for dogs and cats across Australia’s RSPCA network?

Australia’s shelter system is not a single, uniform network. This project follows where pressure appears, when demand changes, and how outcomes differ for dogs and cats.

03 · Where care is physically located

Where are RSPCA facilities located?

The verified RSPCA-operated sites shown here are not evenly distributed across the continent. They are concentrated around major coastal population centres, with much larger gaps across inland Australia.

04 · Not every animal has the same chance

How different are dog and cat outcomes?

Comparing dogs and cats within the same RSPCA jurisdiction reveals differences that a single overall rate would hide. Tasmania shows the widest dog advantage, while Western Australia moves in the opposite direction, with cats recording the higher live-release rate.

05 · What happens after intake

What happens to dogs and cats?

The overall outcome mix differs substantially by species. In 2024–25, almost two-thirds of recorded cat outcomes were rehoming, while dogs were much more likely than cats to be reclaimed or returned to their owner.

64.1% of recorded cat outcomes were rehoming
24.8% of recorded dog outcomes were reunited with owners

Source: RSPCA Australia Annual Statistics 2024–25. Reunited combines the source categories Reclaimed and Returned. Percentages are calculated within each species total and describe the RSPCA reporting network rather than all Australian shelters.

06 · Why euthanasia happens

Why do euthanasia reasons differ?

Among animals euthanased by the RSPCA network in 2024–25, behavioural reasons dominate for dogs, while medical reasons make up the largest share for cats. Looking at composition rather than raw counts makes the species difference easier to see.

59.7% of dog euthanasia cases were recorded as behavioural
46.7% of cat euthanasia cases were recorded as medical

Source: RSPCA Australia Annual Statistics 2024–25. Percentages are calculated within the national dog and cat euthanasia totals reported by the RSPCA network. Categories follow the source report’s reason classifications.

Sources · Method · Acknowledgements

About this visualisation

This project combines official RSPCA reporting with Australian geographic data to explain where shelter pressure appears and how outcomes differ across species and jurisdictions. Additional sources will be added here as the remaining visualisations are completed.

Data sources

RSPCA Australia Annual Statistics 2024–25
State and territory outcomes, live-release calculations, caseload totals and euthanasia reasons.

Australian Bureau of Statistics, ASGS Edition 3
State and Territory geography used for national mapping.

Official RSPCA state and territory location pages
Shelter, animal-care-centre and adoption-centre locations used in the facility map.

Scope and method

RSPCA reporting coverage differs by jurisdiction, so figures on this page describe the RSPCA reporting network rather than every Australian shelter or council pound.

Derived measures, including live-release rate and within-species percentages, are calculated from the published source categories. Definitions are stated beside the relevant charts.
