Behind the Shelter Doors

Where Australia’s animals go when they need a second chance.

Australia’s shelter system is not a single, uniform network. This project follows where pressure appears, when demand changes, and how outcomes differ for dogs and cats.

03 · Where care is physically located

RSPCA facilities cluster around Australia’s populated coast

Physical shelter and adoption facilities are not evenly distributed across the continent. This verified set of RSPCA-operated sites is concentrated around major coastal population centres, with much larger gaps across inland Australia.

Sources: official RSPCA state and territory shelter/location pages and RSPCA Adopt a Pet. 04 · Not every animal has the same chance

A state’s overall result can hide a dog–cat gap

Comparing dogs and cats within the same RSPCA jurisdiction reveals differences that a single overall rate would hide. Tasmania shows the widest dog advantage, while Western Australia moves in the opposite direction, with cats recording the higher live-release rate.

+10.7 pp Tasmania: dog live-release rate above cats
+6.8 pp Western Australia: cat live-release rate above dogs

Source: RSPCA Australia Annual Statistics 2024–25. Live release rate uses the RSPCA definition: rehomed, reclaimed/returned and transferred animals divided by eligible outcomes. Comparisons describe the RSPCA reporting network and should not be interpreted as all shelters in each jurisdiction.
